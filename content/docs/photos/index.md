# Photos

Photos gathers the pictures and videos your account can open, from every drive, into one library. There is no special
photo folder. A picture or video you add in Files, or unpack from a Google Takeout zip, shows up here too.

Videos sit in the same grid as pictures. There is no switch to hide them. A video tile has a play badge, and its
length when Quark could read it. Tap a video and it plays. It streams, so Quark does not download the whole file
first.

An iPhone Live Photo's short motion clip still plays inside the photo viewer. That clip is not listed as its own
video.

You use Photos at home in a web browser, and in the iPhone and Android apps. Away from home, those phone apps reach
your library once an admin turns on remote access in **Settings → Network**. A web browser still works only on your
home WiFi. A big upload goes fastest at home.

Migrating from Google? See the [Google Takeout guide](/docs/files/google-takeout).

## How to use it

1. Open **Photos** from the app menu.
2. Scroll the grid. Months are the headings when you sort by date.
3. Tap **Search** and type part of a file name.
4. Tap a photo to open it. In the viewer you can move to the next photo in this view. Tap a video to play it.
5. On a wide window, tap **Upload** and pick the pictures you want. On a phone, tap the **+** button. Quark does not
   copy your camera roll on its own.

## Browsing

- Photos sit under month headers such as **March 2025**. The header of the month in view stays pinned while you
  scroll. That pinned header is the only month label. A photo with no date sits under **Unknown date**. A video has
  no capture date, so under **Taken** it is filed by the date it was added.
- The sort button picks **Taken**, **Added**, or **Name**, each in both directions. **Taken** uses the capture date
  stored in the photo, and falls back to the date added when there is none. Under a name sort there are no month
  headers. Quark remembers your choice. On a phone, sort is on the second row of the bar.
- On a wide window, the column slider, with **Larger photos** and **Smaller photos** at its ends, changes the grid
  size. On a phone, **Grid size** is in the **Tools** menu.
- **Search** finds every photo and video whose file name contains what you typed. Capitals don't matter. It does not
  stop after a few hundred matches.
- On a phone, the grid is what you see first. Tap the chip that reads **Library**, or the open album's name, to pick
  **All photos**, a category, or an album. On a wide window the albums stay in the sidebar, and the **Showing** list
  there picks **All**, **Quark** (photos on your Quark), **Mobile** (the phone's own library), or **Favorites**.

Pictures that belong to a slideshow are not in this library. Importing a PowerPoint puts them in a folder next to the
slideshow, with `_media` in the folder name. They stay in Files and in the slideshow. Move or rename the slideshow
away from that folder, and those pictures start showing up here.

## Adding photos

1. Open Quark and sign in.
2. Go to **Photos**.
3. Tap **Upload** (or the **+** button on a phone) and pick the photos you want. In a browser you can also drag photos,
   or a folder of them, onto the page.

Upload on this page is for pictures. A video you drop here is skipped, and Quark tells you it is not a photo. Add
videos from [Files](/docs/files). They then show up in this library.

Upload is something you do, one pick at a time. Quark does not back up your camera roll on its own.

- With more than one drive turned on, Quark asks which device the photos go to. They land in the top level of that
  drive.
- A dropped file that is not a photo is left out, and Quark tells you how many were skipped.
- If a photo has the same name as one already there, Quark keeps both.
- With one of your albums open, the photos you upload join that album too.

On an iPhone or iPad, uploading from **Files** can also offer **Photos** as a source, so you do not have to export
into Files first.

## Albums and favorites

Albums and favorites belong to your account. Nobody else sees them, admins included. You can't share an album. Share
the folder that holds the photos instead, from [Files](/docs/files).

- **New album** in the sidebar makes an album. An album's menu has **Rename**, **New sub-album**, and **Delete**.
  Deleting an album keeps the photos.
- An album name must be unique among the albums next to it, ignoring capitals, and can't contain a slash.
- Open an album and tap **Add photos** to pick photos for it. You can also **Select** photos and use **Add to Album**.
  A photo's menu inside an album has **Remove from album**.
- The sort button beside **New album** orders your albums **A–Z**, **Z–A**, **Newest**, or **Oldest**. Favorites and
  the inbox stay on top.

**Favorites** is an album Quark makes for you. **Favorite** on a photo's menu puts it there. **Unfavorite** takes it
out. You can't rename Favorites or put another album inside it.

## Select several photos

Long-press a photo, or tap **Select**, to choose more than one. On a computer, Shift-click a second photo to select
everything between the two. You can also drag a box around tiles. That only adds to the selection, and it stops at
the last photo the grid has loaded.

A bar at the bottom counts what you picked. **Add to Album** stays in the bar. The other actions are **Favorite** or
**Unfavorite**, **Download**, **Share…**, **Make copies**, and **Delete**. On a phone those other actions are in
**More**.

- **Share…** is there only when exactly one photo is selected.
- **Download** saves the selection as one zip named `selection.zip`. More than 2,000 files come as more than one zip.
  A file Quark could not read is listed in `skipped.txt` inside the zip. The zip keeps each file as it is stored. A
  HEIC photo in the zip is still HEIC.
- **Make copies** duplicates the files on your Quark. It is not the clipboard. Quark says how many copies it made.
- **Delete** asks "Move N photos to the trash?" and then moves them to the Trash.
- Inside an album, the bar can **Remove from album**. Inside Favorites, it can **Remove from favorites**.
- Pictures that are still only on a phone, and not on your Quark yet, can be added to an album. The other actions
  stay off until those pictures are on your Quark.

## A photo's menu and the viewer

A photo's menu has **Add to album**, **Favorite** (or **Unfavorite**), **Download**, **Share…**, **Copy**,
**Duplicate**, and **Delete**. **Delete** asks first, then moves the photo to the Trash.

**Copy** puts the picture on the system clipboard, as a PNG, from the web app. Elsewhere the menu says copying a
photo works in the web app for now. With one photo selected, Ctrl+C or Cmd+C does the same copy. **Duplicate** makes
a second file on your Quark and names it.

A video's menu has **Add to album**, **Favorite**, **Download**, **Share**, and **Delete**. It does not offer
**Copy** or **Duplicate**.

Tap a photo to open it. In the viewer you can rotate it 90° clockwise (or press **R**), and the rotation is saved on
your Quark. Moving or renaming the file keeps that rotation. The info panel shows the date and time, the location
and camera when the photo has them, file details, and the albums the photo is in. Swipe onto a video and the viewer
shows a play card. Tap it to play the video.

**Download** saves the file as it is stored. On a phone that file can be a JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP, HEIC, TIFF, BMP, or
DNG, and it goes into Apple Photos or the gallery. Quark says **Downloaded** and the file's name. If you decline
permission, it saves the file another way and tells you why. The details are on [Files](/docs/files#downloading).

A HEIC or HEIF photo also has **Download as JPEG**. That saves a JPEG with the same name, full size and upright, and
keeps the capture date and camera details. The phone or the browser does the conversion. The desktop apps do not
offer it. A browser that cannot read HEIC saves the original and says it could not convert it.

HEIC photos open in the viewer from a preview the app makes and stores on your Quark. If there is no preview yet, the
app makes one first. TIFF, BMP, and raw camera files open too: your Quark converts them for display. The original
stays as it was.

Photos you open are kept on this phone or computer, so the next open is quicker and can work with no connection.
They stay in the Quark app. Clear them, or change how much room they may take, under
[Photos on this device](/docs/settings#photos-on-this-device). A web browser does not show that setting. The browser
keeps its own copies, if it keeps any.

A picture you open from Files, rather than from this library, steps through that folder by name, and shows a
screen-sized preview before the original. See
[A photo opened from Files](/docs/files#a-photo-opened-from-files).

## Duplicates

On All photos, tap **Duplicates**. Quark groups copies as **Identical copies** (the same file) or **Similar photos**
(alike, but maybe edited or resized).

1. Tap a copy to switch it between **Keep** and **Delete**. A group always keeps at least one copy.
2. If the same picture is saved in more than one format, such as HEIC and JPG, **Keep: Any** lets you pick the format
   to keep.
3. Tap **Delete N photos** and confirm. The marked copies move to the Trash. Nothing is deleted for good.

A copy added outside Quark shows up once its thumbnail loads, or after your Quark restarts.

### While Quark is still reading photos

An admin sees a status panel at the top of Duplicates. Everyone else sees nothing there.

The panel is how you tell "no duplicates yet" from "Quark has not finished looking."

- **Still indexing, so results may be incomplete.** A pass is running. It also shows how many photos have been checked
  for duplicates, and how many dates have been read.
- **Indexing paused, so results may be incomplete.** An admin tapped **Pause indexing**. **Resume indexing** continues
  the pass.
- **Not every photo is indexed, so results may be incomplete.** The last pass did not cover the library.
- **All N photos indexed.** The pass finished. The panel names when it last finished.

If some files could not be read, the panel has a button such as **3 photos couldn't be read**. Open it for each file
and a short reason, such as "Can't be read as a photo" or "Too large to check."

## Export an album or the library

**Export** copies pictures and videos out as ordinary files. It does not change the originals or the albums. The
copies do not show up in Photos again. This is not a backup of your Quark.

1. On a wide window, open **Tools** and choose **Export**. On a phone, **Export** is in the **Tools** menu. An album's
   menu has **Export…**, and that starts with that album chosen.
2. The window is titled **Export photos**. It says Quark makes real copies, not links, and that a photo in more than
   one album is copied into each album's folder.
3. Pick **The whole photo library** or **Albums I choose**. The library choice also copies videos, and pictures that
   are in no album, under a folder named **Not in an album**. Each chosen album becomes a folder. A sub-album becomes
   a folder inside it.
4. Pick a drive, open the folder, and tap **Save here**. Then tap **Export**. A folder you cannot add files to is
   refused before the copy starts.
5. Quark makes a new folder inside the one you picked. One album is named after the album. Several albums use
   **Photo albums export**. The whole library uses **Photo library export**. It never replaces a folder that is
   already there.
6. A file named `manifest.csv` goes in the export folder. It lists each copy's path, the drive and path it came
   from, the date taken, the albums, and whether the copy worked.
7. You can close the window. The copy carries on. Follow it in **Jobs**. An admin opens Jobs from **System**. Anyone
   can open it from the jobs badge in the top bar.
8. If some files could not be copied, the window says the export did not finish and names them. It does not say it
   finished when some failed.

## On a spare screen

A photo frame, a tablet, or any spare screen with a web browser can play one of your albums as a slideshow. The page
is small, so a modest screen can run it. Use a browser the screen already has. You do not change the screen's own
software.

This page is for your home WiFi. Remote access reaches Quark from the phone apps, not from a browser, so a frame in
another house will not open it.

1. On the spare screen, open your Quark in its browser, the same way you do at home.
2. At the end of the address, add `/frame`.
3. Sign in with your username and password. On a small screen, signing in can take about ten seconds. The page says
   so while it works.
4. Choose an album. The list is yours alone, and **Favorites** is one of the choices. Each row shows how many photos
   it holds.
5. The screen shows each photo whole, never cropped. It shuffles them, and moves on about every 15 seconds.

Tap the picture to open a menu: **Albums**, **Full screen**, **Sign out**, and **Close**. The menu closes itself
after a few seconds.

The next time you open the page, it starts the album you last picked, as long as you are still signed in. Sign-in
lasts the way it does in the rest of Quark. It ends after 30 days without use, and after 90 days no matter what. The
page remembers your username and the album. It does not save your password.

Sign in as the person whose albums should be on that screen. Anyone who can reach the screen sees that account's
albums. Tap **Sign out** when you are done if other people use the screen.

Raw camera files are shown as ordinary JPEG pictures. A HEIC photo is shown from its stored preview, and one with no
preview yet is skipped. Videos in the album are skipped. If an album has nothing it can show, the page says so.

If the screen can't reach your Quark, it keeps the picture it has, says so, and tries again. If the sign-in has
ended, it goes back to the sign-in form.

If the screen is set to reduce motion, photos change without a fade. The page uses Quark's dark look.

## Limits

- Your Quark won't decode an image over 64 megapixels, such as a stitched panorama. That photo has no thumbnail.
  The original still downloads as it is.
- Search matches file names. It does not look at what is in the picture.
- **Upload** on this page accepts pictures. Videos get into the library when you add them in Files.
- A video tile shows a still when Quark has one. Some videos show only the play badge and the length. The spare-screen
  page still skips videos.

## If something goes wrong

A tile with no picture is not always a missing photo. The badge says which:

- **No preview.** Quark has no thumbnail left to show. The file may have moved or been deleted.
- **Couldn't read this file.** The file looks damaged, or it is not the kind of file its name claims.
- **No preview on this device.** This phone or browser can't picture that format. A video in this state may still
  play, or play on another device.
- **Couldn't load preview.** Quark was busy or refused the thumbnail. The file may be fine. Try again later.

Other snags:

- **The library looks empty.** There are no pictures or videos you can open. Upload one, or check Files.
- **A picture from a slideshow is missing.** It lives in the folder next to the slideshow. Photos leaves that folder
  out on purpose.
- **Duplicates look unfinished.** If you are an admin, read the panel at the top. A pass may still be running. Pause
  it with **Pause indexing** if the Quark is busy with something else, then **Resume indexing**.
- **The frame page asks me to sign in again.** The session ended, or this account has never signed in from the Quark
  app. Sign in once in the app, then try the frame page again.
- **The frame page is blank away from home.** It needs your home WiFi. The phone apps are what remote access is for.
  See [Remote Access](/docs/settings/remote-access).

## Tips

- A big first upload can take a while on WiFi. That's normal.
- Keep Quark powered on and the drive attached while large uploads finish.
- Irreplaceable photos still deserve a second copy. See the [Security guide](/docs/help/security-guide)'s 3-2-1 note.

## Nerd Notes

Checked against quark v0.63.0 (`64f9789e`).

**Library.** `GET /api/v0/photos` lists one page. `q` matches the file name, case-insensitive, and `total` is the
full match count. The old file-name search stopped at 500. This listing pages, so a search of 600 names returns all
of them (`TestPhotoSearch_PagesSixHundredMatches`). Sort keys are `taken`, `added`, and `name`. The app always sends
`includeVideos=true` (`FilesService.getPhotos`). The server default is false, so an older client does not get videos.
A Live Photo's companion video is not a separate row (`hasLiveVideo`). Videos have no EXIF date, so `sort=taken`
files them by the date added. A video tile opens the streamed player (`videoAt`), not the image viewer.

**Selection zip.** `POST /api/v0/files/download-selection` streams 1–2000 files as `selection.zip`. Unreadable,
missing, folder, and detached paths are listed in `skipped.txt`. A name collision gets `(1)`. Photos **Download**
calls `saveSelectionZip`. The Files selection bar does not.

**Export.** Job kind `photo-export` (`pkg/util/albumexportutil`). A library export lists with `IncludeVideos: true`.
`manifest.csv` columns are `file`, `source_drive`, `source_path`, `taken`, `albums`, `result`. Pictures in no album
go under `Not in an album`.

**Clipboard and rotation.** **Copy** writes a PNG whose longest side is at most 2048, and only from the web app.
`fileutil.Move` carries a saved rotation with the file.

**On-device cache.** See [Settings](/docs/settings#nerd-notes). The Photos viewer itself loads the file it will show
(`downloadFileBytes`): a stored HEIC preview, `format=jpeg` for TIFF, BMP, and raw, or the original otherwise. The
screen-sized preview that loads first is the Files viewer, not this one.

**Slideshow media.** `ListPhotos` in `pkg/util/photoutil/list.go` drops any image whose directory is one
`pptxutil.MediaDirsOf` names for a `.qslide`. An import of `Talk.pptx` writes `Talk.qslide` and `Talk_media/`. A
renamed `Talk_(1).qslide` still claims both `Talk_(1)_media` and `Talk_media`. Moving the `.qslide` away leaves the
pictures in the library again.

**Index status.** Admins only. `GET /api/v0/photos/index-status`, `POST /api/v0/photos/index/pause`, and
`POST /api/v0/photos/index/resume`. A non-admin gets 403, and `PhotoIndexStatusSection` renders nothing unless
`AppSettings.isAdmin`. Failure reasons shown in the list come from `Errors.photoIndexFailure`: undecodable,
missing converter, too large, unreadable, or unknown.

**Thumbnails.** `ThumbnailIssue` in `quark_thumbnail_unavailable.dart` maps to the short labels **No preview on this
device**, **Couldn't read this file**, **No preview**, and **Couldn't load preview**. `thumbnailImageExtensions`
includes `.tif`, `.tiff`, and `.bmp`. The server makes a video still for AV1 and VP8 (`videoutil.Keyframe`). Other
codecs, including H.264, HEVC, and VP9, return `ErrNoDecoder`. `NeedsClientRender` is true for every video, so the
thumbnail warmer does not pre-generate them. The uploading client makes a still on iOS, Android, the web app, and
the Linux desktop app (`libmpv`). The macOS and Windows desktop apps do not.

**Decode cap.** `photoutil.MaxDecodePixels` is 64,000,000.

**Frame.** `internal/server/frame` serves `GET /frame` and `/frame/<asset>` as a small static page, ahead of the
Flutter app. It signs in with the same `POST /api/v0/auth/login` as the app and lists `GET /api/v0/albums`, which
creates that account's Favorites album on first use. Address options, read by `frame.js`:

| Option     | Values                              | Default   |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------- | --------- |
| `album`    | Numeric album id                    | Last pick |
| `interval` | Seconds, any integer greater than 0 | 15        |
| `order`    | `shuffle`, `newest`, or `oldest`    | `shuffle` |

`newest` asks `?sort=taken&order=desc`. `oldest` asks `?sort=taken&order=asc`. Shuffle randomizes that list in the
page. Photos matching `.jpg`, `.jpeg`, `.png`, `.gif`, `.webp`, `.bmp`, or `.avif` are shown as they are. `.dng`,
`.cr2`, `.cr3`, `.nef`, `.arw`, `.orf`, `.rw2`, and `.raf` are requested with `format=jpeg`. `.heic` and `.heif` load
`/api/v0/thumbnails/<path>?size=lg`, and one with no stored thumbnail is skipped (quark #3915).
Anything else, including video, is skipped.

The page stores `{ username, album }` in `localStorage` under `quarkFrame`. It does not store the password. The
session cookie is the app's: 30 days, sliding, capped at 90 days from creation (`sessionDuration`,
`SessionMaxLifetime` in `pkg/util/authutil/service.go`). The album list reloads about every 10 minutes. A failed
load retries about every 30 seconds. The on-screen menu hides after about 10 seconds.
`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` drops the cross-fade. A legacy account that has not signed in from the app yet is
told to do that first.

**Downloads.** Same rules as Files: pictures and a short list of video extensions go to the platform photo library
on iOS and Android. See [Files](/docs/files#nerd-notes).
